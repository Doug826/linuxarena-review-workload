# Design and audit supplement

## What can be checked offline

The 200-row honest task table includes a blinded identifier, environment, author-supplied task category, three title indicators, and counts for all 21 half-point score bins. The label s15 denotes 7.5 and s16 denotes 8. Histogram bins recover the pilot thresholds without distributing trajectories, prompts, action text or code from the benchmark. Run verify_offline.py from any directory. It verifies every environment against the original CSV and reruns the original 9,999 task permutations. Original source verification, attack provenance and fingerprinting require the pinned source data. The files in original/ are the contents of the November 19 pilot archive.

## Independent checks

The action-level comparison is a ten-by-two Pearson chi-squared test using above-threshold and remaining actions. Its independence assumption is unsuitable for inference here. The task-level comparison preserves the paired length and alert count of every task and redistributes the 200 tasks into ten groups of 20. The statistic is the sum across environments of squared alert counts divided by scored actions. Subtracting a constant gives the action-weighted squared deviations from the pooled alert rate. This treats tasks as exchangeable under the null; real environments can have different task mixes.

For the illustrative design effect, divide the whole-task sandwich variance of the pooled rate by the independent-action binomial variance. If N is the total exposure, p the pooled rate and G the number of tasks, the numerator is G/(G-1) times the sum of squared (task alerts minus p times task exposure), divided by N squared. This assumes independent tasks and captures combined differences across tasks, including environmental variation. It neither estimates within-task correlation in isolation nor gives the effective sample size for a specific environment or a local cutoff.

To match the external deduplication audit we applied its shell-function and nonempty-argument rules and fingerprinted ordered action content after omitting monitor responses. The source extraction records both inclusion counts. The displayed attack bounds count a flag anywhere in a model-generated trajectory and assume unresolved actions can fall on either side of its threshold. They do not measure whether an alert arrived in time to prevent harm.

## Exploratory task mix

The benchmark supplies six task categories. The three additional indicators use explicit regular expressions on benign task titles, fixed before tabulating their association with alerts. They approximate topics and can miss relevant work. An exposure-adjusted baseline predicts each task's alert count using its number of actions times the source alert rate. Its competitors estimate rates by category, title topic, or both, adding the equivalent of ten source-average tasks at the pooled source rate to each group. Each environment is held out in full. Mean Poisson deviance across the ten equally weighted environments scores predicted mean counts. The interpretation of this score does not require treating action-level outcomes as independent. Realized task length is given to all methods, so this comparison is conditional on exposure.

## Simulation

All November 21 analyses are exploratory after the November 19 results. The model has two beta-binomial stages. It first generates how many of a task's actions score at least 7.5. Conditional on that count it generates how many score strictly above 7.5. Both stages have a normally distributed environment effect on log odds and independent beta-distributed task probabilities. Each task gets its own draws. Parameters are estimated by maximum marginal likelihood using 19-point Gaussian quadrature. The distribution of task lengths is resampled from the observed data. The design simulation assumes lengths are independent of the latent environment and risk given the modeled stages, and ignores source-parameter uncertainty. These are consequential simplifications with only ten source environments.

The deployment decision is the fraction of ties to flag. Always flag scores above 7.5. Flag the fraction of tied actions that makes the predicted rate closest to 1%, clipped to the interval zero to one. Where a raw local sample has no ties, use the affordable endpoint (all ties if its estimated strict rate is within budget). This choice cannot achieve a 1% target in every environment. The pooled estimate uses source population means, the local estimate uses rates observed in the local calibration tasks, and the hierarchical estimate updates the source distribution using the local tasks. In the simulation the pooled and hierarchical methods receive the true source population parameters. This is an optimistic test of a correctly specified source model.

Each scenario contains 500 synthetic studies with ten independent new environments. Methods see the same calibration tasks, test tasks and tie lottery. Simulated studies reuse target environments across choices of sample size for paired design comparisons. We report mean absolute distance from ten alerts per 1,000 under the true simulated environment rates. Separately we test which method has smaller absolute budget error on the finite test batch in each environment. The one-sided exact sign test targets wins in the typical environment and discards ties. A one-sided sign-flip sensitivity analysis targets mean improvement under symmetry of the paired differences. Each test uses a 5% threshold. These are design-specific detection frequencies, not a universal statistical power limit. With 500 studies, Monte Carlo standard error at a 10% detection frequency is about 1.3 percentage points. Additional scenarios remove or strengthen environmental variation while holding marginal base rates fixed; the stronger scenario also allows variation in the strict share of high scores. The 100-test-task design is a prospective sensitivity analysis. Observed benchmark environments only have 20 honest tasks each.

## Prediction intervals on recorded tasks

For each held-out environment we refit both stages on the other nine environments. We drew 16 reproducible local partitions, calibrating on 5 or 10 tasks and predicting the total alerts on all remaining 15 or 10 tasks. Predictive distributions use the observed test lengths and 400 simulated future batches. The pooled forecast draws an environment from the source distribution, the hierarchical forecast updates that distribution with observed local tasks, and the local-only forecast uses half-count-smoothed local probabilities and the source task dispersion. Its plug-in forecast omits local parameter uncertainty. Methods retain their own audit decisions; their interval scores therefore describe forecasting under their respective policies, rather than ranking forecasts for one identical target. All three use a shared tie lottery on the observations. The central 80% interval score equals width plus ten times the distance outside its bounds. Lower is better. Repeated partitions reuse tasks and cannot supply 160 independent replications. Neither the intervals nor the simulation establish a universal minimum local sample size.
